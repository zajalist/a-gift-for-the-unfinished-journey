# Saskarta recorded foley (Freesound CC0)

Made by `Tools/Audio/fetch_foley.py` into `Saved/GameAudio/Recorded/Foley/` (16 WAVs and `manifest.json`).
Import them into `/Game/SaskartaGaea/Audio/Foley`. Every file keeps the name of the synthesized asset it
supersedes, so the import overwrites that SoundWave in place and keeps its references.

    python Tools/Audio/fetch_foley.py            # fetch (cached), process, write, credit, print metrics
    python Tools/Audio/fetch_foley.py --measure  # print metrics of the files already written

All picks are CC0 and use only the HQ OGG previews, decoded to 48 kHz by `freesound_fetch.py`. Each asset is
logged in `freesound_credits.json` and `CREDITS.md` with three alternates. To swap a bad pick, change its
`id` in `PICKS` in the script and run it again.

The synthesized servos, foot plates, idle loop, dock clamp, reconnect and UI sounds are unchanged and stay
in `Saved/GameAudio/Foley/` (generated by `make_foley_sfx.py`).

## Layered (recording plus the existing synth, same length)

The synth file is read from `Saved/GameAudio/Foley/` and the result is written to `Recorded/Foley/`, so
running the script again never layers twice. The recording is scaled against the synth's RMS, summed with it,
given 5 ms fades and peak-normalised to -3 dBFS.

| Asset | Recorded layer | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| `SFX_Carrier_Collapse` (2.5 s) | Big falling debris (crash) (703247): tiles, stone and metal on concrete | The main crash moves from 0.26 s to 0.50 s, the synth's body slam. A 50 Hz notch removes a +10 dB mains peak in the source. 0.4 s tail fade, -2 dB relative to the synth. |
| `SFX_Capsule_Scrape_Failing` (3 s) | kettlebell on concrete rock stone metal drag (545544) | Uses the most continuous 3 s of drag (14.75 to 17.75 s). The layer is multiplied by the synth's 30 ms envelope raised to the power 0.8, so it follows the stick-slip, the motor sag and the 1.2 s drop-out. -3 dB relative to the synth. |
| `SFX_Capsule_Hatch_Open` (2 s) | Metallic Groan (496836): heavy bunker door, rusty hinges | The loudest 1.1 s of groan (4.55 to 5.65 s), highpassed at 80 Hz, with an 80 ms fade in and a 150 ms fade out. It is placed at 0.40 to 1.50 s, so it ends before the hatch hits its stop at 1.55 s. -4 dB relative to the synth. |

Hum check: on the full source recordings, the narrow peak at 50, 60, 100 or 120 Hz is at most +2.4 dB over its
neighbours. The two exceptions are 703247 (+9.9 dB at 50 Hz, notched out) and 496836 (+17.9 dB at 120 Hz). The
120 Hz peak is the groan's own pitch in an Australian recording, where mains is 50 Hz, so it was left alone.
The script also prints a hum figure for each output file. For one-shots under 1 s that figure is 3 to 15 dB,
but it is periodogram variance from a single short frame, not hum. On the loops it is 1.6 to 1.8 dB.

## Least-sure picks (nobody has listened yet)

1. **`SFX_WalkingAid_Tap_Gravel_01..04`**: an iPhone voice-memo recording (AGC) of a blind walker, so the
   recordist's own gravel footsteps are in the file. The picks are the sharpest transients, but 01 (67 ms decay)
   and 03 (60 ms) could be footstep crunches, not cane hits. Alternates: 769994, which is one tap sample
   repeated and was rejected for that reason; 739192; and 157887.
2. **`SFX_Capsule_Tow_Dirt_Loop`**: chained from 7 pulls of a stone slab, so it may sound rhythmic or pulsed
   rather than like a continuous drag. It is scrape-heavy for dirt even after the 5 kHz lowpass.
3. **`SFX_Capsule_Tow_Gravel_Loop`**: a wheeled suitcase pulled by a walker, so the walker's footsteps may be
   audible. It is also lighter than a capsule; pitching it down about 10 to 15 % in the cue would add weight.
4. **`SFX_WalkingAid_Tap_Stone_01..04`**: an aluminium white cane on asphalt gives a light, bright tick
   (centroid 2.2 to 3.4 kHz), not the knock of a wooden stick on rock. No CC0 wooden stick-on-stone recording
   was found.
5. **`SFX_Cloth_Rustle_01..03`**: chosen automatically from a clip that also contains throws and pickups.
   The crest-factor filter should exclude those, but it is unverified.
